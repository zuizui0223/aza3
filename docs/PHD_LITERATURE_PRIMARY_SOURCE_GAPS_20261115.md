## 0. Executive finding: which novelty claims must be dropped
1. **Phylogenomic diversification and niche analysis for Cirsium already exists.** Moreyra et al. 2025 [10.1016/j.ympev.2025.108285](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ympev.2025.108285) analyzed 299 individuals/251 taxa with 350 target-capture nuclear loci; hybridization/ILS complicate the tree. Siniscalchi et al. 2023 [10.1086/724310](https://doi.org/10.1086/724310) analyzed 64 North American Cirsium taxa with time-scaled tree/habitat/precipitation/pollinator syndrome and diversification. They report a precipitation/semiaridity association with diversification but no detected morphological influence on **species diversification rate** (distinct from per-lineage phenotypic rate or adaptive fitness).
2. **Multiple selective agents acting on bracts and different developmental stages are already a literature-level framework**, explicitly in Song et al. 2024 Biological Reviews [10.1111/brv.13060](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.13060). Do not claim discovery of bract multifunctionality, phenology-dependent pressures or generic pollination-defense conflict.
3. **Direct Asteraceae capitulum-angle and phyllary-defense experiments already exist.** Chen et al. [10.1080/17550874.2012.702793](https://doi.org/10.1080/17550874.2012.702793) manipulated nodding vs erect in Cremanthodium, reported reduced achenes in erect and UV/rain pollen protection but no pollinator preference. Wu & Yahara 2017 [10.1007/s11284-017-1444-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11284-017-1444-5) removed recurved outer phyllaries in Taraxacum and observed different slug barriers. **Neither is a true Cirsium phyllary-spine experiment nor a multi-lineage O×spine gene-history study.**
4. **Phylogenetic florivory patterns by bud/flower/fruit stage in Japanese Asteraceae are published.** Oguro & Sakai 2015 [10.3732/ajb.1400233](https://doi.org/10.3732/ajb.1400233): 18 wild species, morphology and biochemistry with phylogenetic GLMM; head diameter/number, water, N, P, phenolics relate to florivory with stage-specific directions. Head size, resource quality and head number are mandatory competing explanations, not nice-to-have controls.
5. **Cirsium pollinator versus enemy effects are already known to vary by context.** Ohashi & Yahara 1998 [10.2307/2446309](https://doi.org/10.2307/2446309) measured pollinator responses to flower display. Ohashi & Yahara 2000 [10.1139/b99-182](https://doi.org/10.1139/b99-182) connected flower production and predation to reproduction (independent studies/cohorts, don't subtract as same-head selection gradients). Thomas 2003 [10.22543/0090-0222.2085](https://doi.org/10.22543/0090-0222.2085) found no detected benefit of sticky exudates when neutralized in one study. Reville et al. 2026 [10.3390/insects17101015](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects17101015) documented distinct tephritid guilds and seed outcomes in C. funkiae (3 sites, **9 plants, 317 heads**, not 317 independent plants, one season).

**Defensible novelty remaining within searched sources (not an exhaustive priority-of-discovery claim):** no checked direct study jointly identifies (i) independent genomic origin/ancestral transition of *true capitulum gravity angle + true phyllary spine/opening*, (ii) ontogenetic trajectory from *same marked head*, (iii) real pollinator and ovipositor access pathways by local guild, and (iv) independent site/plant-level viable-achene functional consequences. The narrow contribution is *lineage-conditioned functional reassembly* of the same capitulum architecture, **not** first-ever climate adaptation, bract defense or multiple selection agents.

## 2. Revise central hypotheses after reading these papers
- **D1: transition rather than higher speciation**: Japanese Cirsium O and B can show different ancestral transition numbers/positions without either increasing species diversification. Competing H0: joint distributions are explained by common history + species sex/head size/phenology + genetic admixture.
- **D2: a joint trait/effect map, not just climate correlations**. Map sampled head states (O at bud/anthesis/fruit; bract opening and actual spine length), local precipitation **during anthesis**, environment available range, and field guild. PGLS / BM baseline; OU1 / regime OU only if independent regime contrasts and measured power; moving-optimum OU is optional and not a requirement.
- **D3: environmental routes vs partner routes**. Weather/microclimate → wetting → pollen viability; true O×B×guild → entry/contact → confirmed oviposition and pollen transfer → filled viable achenes. Confounders: species/sex, head number/diameter, total florets, bud stage, plant size/resources, geography, phenology, insects' observation opportunity. Manipulate O versus sham first, then one non-damaging B access intervention *only if* partner and permission gates pass.
- **Developmental distinction**: `O_bud`, `O_anthesis`, `O_fruit` are **repeated measures on the same ID**, not three independent species' traits. The same anatomical head may bear positive, neutral or negative effects depending on when guilds occur; historical functionality (exaptation) requires further evidence beyond present-day multiple roles.
- **New strongest potential result**: on independently evolved lineages, O and B can differ in history, yet actual *conditional* marginal fitness response of O depends on B and realized guild; a predeclared holdout natural contrast reproduces a predicted sign or conditional-response curve. This is stronger than showing multiple selected tests in different species; **with only a few independent origins a Nature-scale universal claim cannot be identified**.

## 3. Comparative-method guardrails
- **OU small-tree failure**: Cooper et al. (2016) [10.1111/bij.12701](https://doi.org/10.1111/bij.12701) shows likelihood ratio OU/BM model preference vulnerable to small clades and measurement error. Simulate model adequacy/power on actual tree + sampling coverage + trait error before comparing multiple-regime models; report broad uncertainty rather than invented optimum.
- **Discordant genealogies**: Hibbins, Breithaupt & Hahn (2023) [10.1073/pnas.2220389120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220389120) demonstrates trait-rate/ASR biases from using one species tree when locus trees disagree. Existing EAzami Cirsium tree ambiguity and RAD locus dropout warrant an ensemble, not single trusted RAD tip tree.
- **Modern interpretation**: Pienaar et al. (2026) [10.1093/jeb/voaf113](https://doi.org/10.1093/jeb/voaf113) reviews moving adaptive optima and inertia; it offers **optional advanced** environmental-predictor models rather than proof of causal adaptation. Do not interpret OU alpha as empirical selection coefficient.
- **Historical time**: Moreyra dated **published chronology** is not the same as recovering the exact time-scaled tree Newick plus calibrated nodes. Until recovered/independently validated, distinguish raw branch lengths from Myr and avoid transition-per-Myr or time-lag estimates.
- **Niche data**: sample *realized flowering niche* (precipitation, sunlight, temperature, actual insect availability); test *climatic niche* and *realized interacting guild* separately. Do not infer local guild from species-level GloBI presence.

## 2026-11-15 additional source-contrast: sticky bract defense may vary across cohorts
- **Willson, Anderson & Thomas 1983**, DOI [10.2307/2425228](https://doi.org/10.2307/2425228): secondary indexed original abstract reports greater seed predation after experimental occlusion of sticky exudate in `C. discolor` in Illinois 1980, **but no detected effect** in `C. flodmanii` in Minnesota 1980/1982. Original full text, sample sizes and per-head data were **not** authenticated in this review; it is secondary-index confirmation, not a new verified pooled treatment coefficient. The response contrasts with the later Thomas 2003 reported no-detected-benefit experiment; study location, period, guild and methodology differ, so **do not claim controlled ecological sign reversal**.
- **Thomas 2007**, DOI [10.22543/0090-0222.2188](https://doi.org/10.22543/0090-0222.2188): primary journal-archived author abstract reports pollinators did not contact sticky traps, some seed predators avoided traps, and ants/aphids were deterred; predators such as spiders and minute pirate bugs sometimes used traps. Thus presence of sticky bract tissue is not proof of physical exclusion of seed predators, and access-route measurement must precede an adaptive-function claim.
- **Implication for the PhD**: analyze true spines separately from stickiness; stratify verified enemy guild; use matched/sham treatments and same-head immature damage to mature viable-achene measurements, not an asserted universal 'spines protect from florivores'.
