# aza3 proposed pivot: capitulum defense–attraction evolution using RAD-seq and fitness experiments

**Status:** proposed scientific direction / draft PR (2026-10-08). **No collection, field manipulation, tissue sampling or additional sequencing is authorized by this document.**

## 1. Scientific question

> **How have composite flower heads evolved to protect reproductive tissues without forfeiting effective pollination, and do changes in the arrangement of head components mitigate, reverse or intensify that functional conflict?**

The evolutionary object is the **whole capitulum**, and its potential causal components are (a) involucre/phyllaries/physical protrusions and exudates, (b) accessible and visible florets, floral optical display and rewards, (c) whole-head spatial geometry, and (d) flowering/developmental stage.

The head is not assumed to be an optimized defense device. For any given structure, defense and attraction are **functions to demonstrate**, not labels inferred from anatomy.

### Scope decision (replaces the proposed WGS-centered flagship question)

- **RAD-seq has a single bounded role:** recover ancestry, population history, phylogenetic contrasts and sensitivity to admixture / ploidy. It is **not** used to identify shape, spine, color, orientation or stickiness genes. No GWAS, WGS, haplotype-age or regulatory rescue is needed for the primary paper.
- Study capitulum and component evolution **and** field functional payoffs. Do not split into a pure phylogenetic-pattern paper and an unrelated ecological-effects paper.
- Reversibility, latent pathway reactivation and genome-wide reconfigurable inheritance are **not prerequisites**. Existing research branches remain preserved as separate exploratory programmes; they are not evidence for this study.
- No claim of unusually fast evolution without a calibrated dated-tree ensemble.

## 2. Distinguishing, falsifiable hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Evolutionary / ecological prediction | Falsifier or weaker outcome |
|---|---|---|
| H1: genuine defense–pollination conflict | Strengthening a candidate defensive barrier reduces florivore/enemy access **but also** reduces *effective* pollination under matched conditions; fitness optimum depends on both visitor guilds | Only enemy response, only pollinator response, or no demonstrable costs; a simple trade-off is not established |
| H2: division of labor / mitigation by component combinations | Protective involucre and exposed/rewarding florets jointly permit high enemy exclusion *and* effective pollination. A predeclared component interaction predicts viable achene output beyond single-trait effects; favorable combinations occur in more than one robust RAD-phylogenetic contrast | Component combinations follow ancestry alone or fail to shift both channels; an apparent non-additivity disappears after sham, size, season and site checks |
| H3: selective filtering rather than generalized deterrence | A barrier disproportionately blocks antagonists, robbers or ineffective visitors while retaining effective pollinators. **Total visits can fall while viable seed output rises.** The direction depends on locally measured visitor behavior, not taxonomic guild names alone | Blocked and admitted visits show the same per-visit contribution, or net seed benefit is absent |
| H0: ancestry, development or abiotic sorting without demonstrated adaptation | Head states change with taxon history, flowering stage, head size, wetting or geography; no replicated defense/attraction fitness advantage is identified | Replicated causal effects plus phylogenetically independent, ecology-predicted combinations challenge this null |

H2 and H3 are stronger biological outcomes than merely confirming that defended heads deter herbivores. Neither is asserted in advance.

## 3. Three linked evidence layers

### A. RAD-seq evolutionary backbone

Use an **all-Japan / Ryukyu ancestry backbone as access allows** with one comparable reduced-representation assay, vouchers, individual phenotype and cytotype QC. Preserve the existing Chapter 3 RAD-seq design constraints. Characterize within-taxon population structure and potential reticulation before interpreting trait transitions. Analyze alternative admissible trees/networks; do not treat a single maximum-likelihood topology as history certainty.

Map independently measured, **functionally neutral** anatomical states:
- involucre geometry: phyllary posture, opening/access gap, projection length and placement, gland/exudate status, actual hardness or spine-tip measurements when feasible;
- advertisement/reward: visible floret area, optical color, number of open florets, nectar, head-scale display;
- developmental stage: bud, anthesis and seed maturation; orientation only as a stage/geometry covariate unless the focal question requires it.

Ask whether *changes in combinations* (not just univariate transitions) appear repeatedly on alternative topologies, and whether transitions relate to independently observed visitor/antagonist regimes. Do not reconstruct a "defense trait" from image-based spine-like projection alone.

### B. Present-day head-level functional ecology

On the **same marked individual and head**, record:
- visitor approaches, landings, pollen-related reproductive contact and (on a subset) actual pollen deposition; rates per observation hour and contribution per legitimate visit;
- visitor behavior (pollen delivery, nectar robbing, florivory, seed predation, predation/parasitoid) **before** assigning mutualist versus antagonist roles;
- damage at bud, anthesis and pre-dispersal stages; entry attempts and successful enemy access;
- weather, site, height, head size, floret availability, phenological stage and plant vigor;
- filled and viable achenes per head, preferably relative to initial ovule/floret potential; pollen-supplemented and bagged controls when separately authorized.

Capture the counterfactual distinction: an increase in head visitation is not automatically better pollination, and a reduction in enemy visits is not automatically increased fitness. Leaf herbivory, internal seed predators, large vertebrate feeding and weather damage should not be collapsed into one generic enemy measure.

### C. Non-destructive field causality, after authorization

**First technical pilot:** reuse the existing EAzami JPN36/Cirsium sieboldii external phyllary-access proxy versus handling-matched sham, *only if* field, conservation, manipulation and terminal collection rights are independently granted. The existing pilot is **technical feasibility**, not an efficacy study. Protected/small populations are not fallback candidates for convenience.

**Successor confirmatory test:** replicate a preregistered qualified barrier/access perturbation across populations or independent abundant systems. Primary paired mediators are effective pollination and enemy access/damage; primary endpoint is **viable achenes**. Count individual plants as experimental replicates, not observation bouts. Include sham, injury/microclimate and head-orientation QC.

**Mitigation interaction:** estimate whether naturally measured advertisement/exposure modifies the barrier's effect on both channels. This is observational interaction evidence unless floret display is also manipulated cleanly and independently. A fully randomized 2 × 2 access × advertisement design is an optional later proof, *not* assumed easy/safe.

Stratify prospectively across measured enemy and pollinator availability. A null test under almost no enemy exposure does not establish that defense is useless; a low-pollinator period cannot evaluate attraction benefits. Species with insufficient exposure provide explicit **NOT_IDENTIFIABLE** results.

## 4. Analytic separation

**Historical statement:** repeated transitions / remappings with RAD tree and network uncertainty, taxon-level observation coverage and trait-state uncertainty.

**Ecological association:** trait combinations × visitor behavior × stage and enemy regime with site/population and size controls, plus phylogenetic covariance for among-taxon contrasts.

**Causal field statement:** randomized treatment versus sham effects on visitor-specific access, damage, pollen transfer and viable achenes; mediation language limited by experimental identification.

**Adaptive significance:** at least one focal repeated fitness advantage from a rigorously controlled manipulation, appropriately bounded to the tested site, visitor regime and developmental stage. A hypothesis of repeated adaptive evolution additionally requires replicated historically independent contrasts with ecological convergence; it is not proven from parallel trait states alone.

## 5. Data already in hand and how to use them

- **Azami:** image traits for floral lightness/chroma/hue, head form, involucre shape and projection prominence/pattern. These are morphology measurements, **not** defense strength, anatomical spine length, nectar or floral function. Use them for phenotype-space exploration and image-calibration targets, then collect direct anatomy and functional ecology.
- **EAzami:** Japanese-radiation topology-conditioned minima already documented for orientation, phyllary posture and stickiness, with unequal-depth histories; they motivate a **RAD-supported** ancestry update, not independently established adaptive origins.
- **EAzami JPN36:** a non-destructive external phyllary-access proxy and sham are technically specified, but field execution is not authorized. It may become a defensible pilot after all rights, conservation and device gates.
- **aza3 current Chapter 3:** same-individual RAD-seq / phenotype / cytotype / voucher routing, taxonomic uncertainty and anti-overclaiming guardrails survive unchanged.

## 6. Minimum design before substantial new sampling

| Route | Minimum proof target | Disposition if not met |
|---|---|---|
| Historical | More than one credible, topology-robust change in head-component configuration; sufficient population-level phenotype verification | Describe bounded history, not a recurrent adaptive syndrome |
| Ecological | Same head/plant linked to both visitor and enemy channels, plus stage and abiotic exposure | Morphological correlation only |
| Functional | Authorized, qualified active treatment + matched sham + terminal seed outcome; follow-up replicated beyond pilot | State functional feasibility/effect only, not adaptation |
| Mechanistic mitigation | Defensive access and advertised/rewarding floral component contribute distinct and jointly favorable pathways; confounding addressed | Do not claim trade-off mitigation |
| Comparative synthesis | Historically independent contrasts AND measured fitness/environmental conditions consistent in direction | Limit conclusion to focal populations and head-scale function |

Sample sizes for the confirmatory field study must be based on baseline encounter rates, estimated seed variance and detectable effect size. The existing 12-pair JPN36 device pilot is *not* a powered causal test, and inherited 298-person WGS-oriented allocations are not to be treated as mandatory sample counts for this revised question.

## 7. Three possible main results — only one has to win

1. **Defense–pollination conflict is conditional:** the same structure helps under heavy antagonism but becomes costly when effective pollinators are scarce. The fitness optimum moves across visitor communities.
2. **Adaptive mitigation:** heads with strong protective envelopes and independently exposed floral display maintain both services; topology-robust repeated combinations and experiment-backed payoffs connect ecology to evolution.
3. **Selective exclusion:** apparently costly barriers are actually ecological filters. Fewer visitors can produce more viable seeds when visitors differ in reproductive benefit or harm.

The most novel synthesis is **not** "spines defend", "flowers attract", or "traits evolved independently": it is **how the composite head changes the *shape of the conflict* across visitor communities and how those functional solutions have evolved more than once**.

## 8. Claim and safety limits

- No genome-wide shape/spine/orientation causation claims.
- No "reversal" or regulatory reactivation inferred from RAD or phenotype alone.
- No forcing island herbivore release, pollinator absence or visitor antagonism without observational support.
- No functional proxy measured from images admitted as observed selection.
- No equivalence claimed from nonsignificant differences or weak encounter rates.
- No field work, collection, sticky-compound neutralization, exclusion, terminal head harvest or viability assays without specific permissions. Keep deidentified locality/permit records.
- No claim that adaptive function today identifies the original selective pressure during a historical trait change.

## 9. Relevant precedents

- Agrawal et al. (2000), *Southwestern Naturalist* 45:1–5. Spines of *Centaurea solstitialis* differentially affect legitimate and illegitimate visitors, and clipping lowered filled-seed percentage. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/3672545 . Already demonstrates one-case visitor filtering: do **not** market filtering alone as unprecedented.
- Ohashi, Jürgens & Thomson (2021), *Biological Reviews* 96:2258–2280. Distinguishes visitor-mediated phenotypic versus opportunity trade-offs and proposes trade-off mitigation and adaptive generalization. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12754 . A mechanism must be *tested*, not assumed from this framework.
- *Let’s pluck the daisy* (2023), *Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society*: capitulum and involucre architecture have both display and protection implications. https://academic.oup.com/botlinnean/article/201/4/391/7010533 .
- *Defensive functions and potential ecological conflicts of floral stickiness* (2022), *Scientific Reports*. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-23261-2 . Floral adhesives can affect both florivory and mutualists in a context-dependent fashion.
- *Cumulative herbivory outpaces compensation for early floral damage on a monocarpic perennial thistle* (2017), *American Journal of Botany*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29218537/ . A direct *Cirsium* example of fitness consequences of repeated floral damage.
