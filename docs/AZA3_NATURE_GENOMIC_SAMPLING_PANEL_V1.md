# aza3 Nature genomic sampling panel v1

**Status:** strategic sampling design — not collection authorization  
**Date:** 2026-10-04

## Goal

Sample populations that discriminate the genomic source of repeated capitulum innovation rather than merely maximizing taxonomic coverage.

The panel tests four competing source models:

- repeated de novo mutation;
- ancestral standing variation;
- introgressive reuse;
- polyploid/homeolog/regulatory reuse.

It also creates the same-individual data needed to test whether different capitulum modules carry separable genomic histories.

## Phase A — primary discovery panel

The existing recommended Japan/Ryukyu population-genomic panel is retained at **298 individuals**. Its role is changed from generic population history to hypothesis discrimination.

## Why C. sieboldii is now central

EAzami authority evidence places *C. sieboldii* at:

- downward/nodding orientation, with local upright forms explicitly noted;
- appressed phyllaries;
- nonsticky or nearly nonsticky involucre;
- documented white morph variation;
- diploid taxon-level cytotype context.

This makes *C. sieboldii* the strongest current system in which several capitulum modules may segregate inside one species.

The Nature-scale question is not whether all these traits share one locus. It is the opposite:

> do colour, orientation and protective-envelope traits associate with different genomic regions/local ancestries, allowing novel combinations to be assembled by recombination and ancestry sorting?

All module states must be measured on the same individuals rather than inferred from species-level authority descriptions.

## Phase B — molecular flagship validation

### Cirsium japonicum var. takaoense

Six public transcriptome individuals already have recovered W/BP morph labels (3 white, 3 bluish-purple). They are a discovery/anchor dataset, not population replication.

Before new sequencing:

1. reanalyse the existing six labelled transcriptomes for topology/gene-tree discordance without converting geography into morph causation;
2. freeze the candidate population-genomic hypotheses;
3. only then open new dense W/BP sampling where permissions/collaboration make it feasible.

Preferred new design if opened:

- 20–30 white individuals;
- 20–30 bluish-purple individuals;
- >=2 populations per state where possible;
- mixed populations preferred when available;
- matched cytotype, floral RNA, pigment chemistry and standardized colour.

This system is the strongest molecular test of regulatory reuse, but it is not required to delay the Japan Phase-A panel.

## Phase C — reserve replication systems

### Cirsium aomorense

Known colour polymorphism is compressed at species-tip resolution and the current Moreyra sample is not morph-linked.

Open only if:

- current W/C populations can be verified;
- Phase-A colour systems do not already discriminate the source models;
- same-individual morph + genome + cytotype sampling is feasible.

### Orientation reserve set

If within-*sieboldii* orientation variation is insufficient, choose an independent downward/upward contrast only after the dated tree and current population availability are frozen.

Current authority-coded downward candidates include *C. kasaianum*, *C. norikurense*, *C. yezoense*, *C. kamtschaticum*, *C. sieboldii* and *C. pendulum*.

Do not nominate a convenient species pair after seeing genomic similarity.

## Mandatory same-individual package

Every Phase-A individual must link:

- immutable individual ID;
- accepted/source taxon name;
- population ID and deidentified locality key;
- voucher or voucher-linked diagnostic images;
- standardized visible flower colour;
- gravity-referenced capitulum orientation where flowering material permits;
- direct phyllary posture and quantitative architecture where scorable;
- stickiness/gland/exudate state where scorable;
- leaf DNA sample and library ID;
- cytotype / relative genome size or explicit missingness;
- plastid companion marker/haplotype where informative;
- collection/authorization IDs.

Do not substitute species-level authority states for an individual's phenotype in genotype–phenotype analyses.

## Tiered genomic design

### Tier 1 — all 298 Phase-A individuals

Use one common population-genomic assay for ancestry triage:

- population structure;
- within-system ancestry;
- admixture/contact;
- cytotype-aware genotype QC;
- same-individual phenotype–ancestry association;
- selection of replicated transitions for deep sequencing.

Tier 1 may be RAD/GBS-like or another common reduced-representation design. It is not sufficient for causal haplotype-age or fine-scale local-genealogy claims.

### Tier 2 — dense resequencing of selected systems

Selection must be frozen after Tier-1 QC but before fine-scale outcome inspection.

Tier-2 targets should maximize discrimination among:

- de novo mutation;
- standing variation;
- introgressive reuse;
- regulatory/polyploid reuse.

Required analyses can include:

- local genealogy/topology weighting;
- haplotype sharing;
- D/f-statistics or equivalent gene-flow tests;
- local ancestry;
- relative allele-age inference where defensible;
- trait association with population-structure control;
- structural-variant follow-up where necessary.

## Cross-module combinatorial test

At least one focal system must score multiple capitulum modules on the same individuals.

Primary candidate: *C. sieboldii*.

Support for genomic combinatorial reuse requires more than multiple trait associations.

The strongest pattern is:

1. module A and module B associate with different genomic regions or local ancestries;
2. those genomic histories are at least partly separable across individuals;
3. recombinant/admixed individuals carry novel combinations of the modules;
4. the combination pattern cannot be explained by one whole-genome ancestry axis alone.

## Representative ecology/fitness chains

Genomic history and adaptive function remain separate estimands.

Priority causal exemplar:

- orientation × rain/wetting → wetness/pollen or stigma process → filled viable achenes.

Additional exemplars:

- JPN15 stickiness neutralization → arthropod-guild filtering → viable seed;
- JPN36 phyllary access manipulation → antagonist access/damage → viable seed;
- colour → pigment/optical phenotype, then signalling versus radiation/thermal mechanism.

## Sample-size boundary

The Phase-A n values are population-history and ancestry targets inherited from the prior plan. They are not guaranteed to identify causal loci.

Before any genome-wide association or rare-variant claim:

- perform outcome-blind power/precision simulations under the actual ploidy, allele-frequency and population structure;
- downgrade to ancestry/local-genealogy inference if locus-level power is insufficient;
- never rescue power by pooling biologically incompatible cytotypes or populations.

## Stop rules

- No field collection is authorized by this document.
- No post-hoc replacement of inaccessible populations after their expected genomic leverage is known.
- No diploid-only joint caller across mixed cytotypes.
- No species-level phenotype substituted for same-individual phenotype.
- No RAD-only causal haplotype or allele-age claim.
- No claim that all modules use the same genomic source.
- No adaptive-introgression claim without phenotype and representative fitness evidence.
