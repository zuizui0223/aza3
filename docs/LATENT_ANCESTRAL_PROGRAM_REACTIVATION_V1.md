# aza3 latent ancestral-program reactivation hypothesis v1

Status: downstream mechanism hypothesis; it does not replace the F/M/R inheritance-unit test.

## Question

When a capitulum state appears repeatedly, is it repeatedly invented, repeatedly imported, or repeatedly reactivated from an ancestral developmental program that remained latent after phenotypic loss?

## Why this is distinct from ordinary repeated evolution

Repeated phenotype does not identify mechanism.

At least five mechanistic routes can produce the same apparent recurrence:

1. de novo parallelism — independent new mutations repeatedly create a similar phenotype;
2. cryptic/standing variation release — pre-existing alleles become phenotypically expressed under a new genetic or ecological context;
3. latent ancestral-program reactivation — the generative pathway or GRN for the ancestral phenotype persisted after visible loss but was transcriptionally or developmentally suppressed, then re-opened;
4. hemiplasy / introgressive reacquisition — the state or its genetic basis persists in another lineage and re-enters through reticulation;
5. non-homologous reconstruction — an ancestral-like phenotype is rebuilt by a different molecular/developmental route.

Only route 3 is the strict suppressed-pathway re-expression hypothesis.

## Strong prediction of latent-program reactivation

A true reactivation event should show molecular/developmental continuity across the A -> B -> A-like transition.

The derived/lost state B should retain much of the ancestral generative machinery even when the phenotype is absent.

Expected signatures include:
- coding integrity of key pathway genes is retained during the phenotypic-loss phase;
- retained genes remain under purifying constraint or are maintained because they function in another tissue/trait;
- the lost state shows pathway suppression at regulatory/expression level rather than wholesale pathway destruction;
- regained individuals/species restore expression of the same downstream pathway or GRN in the same tissue/developmental stage;
- a small number of regulatory changes can restore the state;
- restored phenotype chemistry/development resembles the ancestral program more than an independently reconstructed phenotype.

## Flower-colour precedent

Anthocyanin systems provide a direct precedent.

In Iochrominae, repeated losses of floral anthocyanin pigmentation are associated with regulatory down-regulation of late anthocyanin-pathway genes while substantial pathway function remains conserved. At least one lineage appears to have regained floral anthocyanins after loss, consistent with renewed floral activation of the retained pathway.

The important biological point is not that colour can reverse.

It is that a pathway can remain evolutionarily available while being phenotypically silent.

## Extension from colour to the whole capitulum

The stronger aza3 question is whether this principle applies to multiple capitulum components.

A head may contain a mixture of:
- reactivated ancestral component programs;
- continuously retained modules;
- newly derived components;
- introgressed components.

Therefore an ancestral-looking capitulum need not represent whole-organ reversal.

It may be a mosaic of reactivated ancestral programs and derived modules.

This creates a direct bridge to reconfigurable integration:

latent ancestral programs -> component-specific reactivation -> new module combinations -> reconfigured capitulum -> repeated / reversal-like phenotypes

## What would count as strong evidence in Cirsium

For a focal component, classify evidence across five layers.

### L1 — ancestral state

Phylogenetic reconstruction supports that the phenotype was present before a lineage-specific loss/suppression event.

### L2 — machinery persistence through the loss state

During the apparent absence of the trait:
- structural genes remain intact;
- no widespread pseudogenization/deletion destroys the pathway;
- pathway genes remain constrained and/or expressed in another tissue or function.

### L3 — suppression

The derived state shows a regulatory mechanism capable of silencing the focal developmental/biochemical program, for example:
- loss of an activator;
- gain/up-regulation of a repressor;
- cis-regulatory shutdown;
- tissue/stage-specific chromatin closure;
- developmental termination or apoptosis of a retained precursor.

### L4 — reactivation

The recurrent/regained state restores the same pathway in the relevant tissue/stage:
- re-expression of the same downstream genes;
- restoration of the same biochemical intermediates or developmental sequence;
- regulatory change consistent with release from suppression.

### L5 — perturbation / rescue

Strongest evidence:
- restoring the relevant regulator/pathway in the suppressed state recreates the ancestral-like component; and/or
- suppressing it in the regained state recreates the loss state.

L5 may be difficult in non-model thistles and is not required for the first genomic test, but it defines the mechanistic ceiling.

## Data hierarchy for aza3

### Colour

Best first candidate because anthocyanin biology is tractable.

Collect, where possible:
- floral pigment chemistry;
- floral RNA;
- leaf RNA or other tissue where the pathway may remain active;
- genomic coding integrity of pathway genes;
- MYB/bHLH/WD40 regulatory candidates;
- ancestral-state reconstruction across the relevant clade.

A white -> coloured recurrence supports reactivation only if the white state retains the pathway and the recurrent coloured state restores homologous floral expression.

### Orientation

Do not assume an on/off pathway.

Test whether recurrent orientation states involve:
- the same developmental growth asymmetry / peduncle program;
- homologous hormone or polarity networks;
- stage-specific expression differences;
- repeated release of a retained developmental program rather than independent mechanical changes.

### Phyllary / involucre

Use developmental-stage imaging and floral/involucre transcriptomics to test whether recurrent states reuse the same developmental network.

## Mechanism decision classes

REACTIVATION_STRONG
- ancestral-state support;
- persistence of relevant machinery through loss;
- regulatory/developmental suppression in the loss state;
- homologous pathway reactivation in the recurrent state;
- no better explanation by introgression or non-homologous reconstruction;
- perturbation/rescue strongly preferred.

REACTIVATION_SUPPORTIVE
- persistence + suppression + regained homologous expression supported, but direct causal rescue missing.

STANDING_VARIATION_RELEASE
- recurrence derives from cryptic/standing variation, but continuity of one suppressed ancestral developmental program is not established.

INTROGRESSIVE_REACQUISITION
- relevant haplotype/program is reacquired from another lineage.

DE_NOVO_PARALLELISM
- independent new mutations produce the phenotype without retained ancestral-program continuity.

NON_HOMOLOGOUS_RECONSTRUCTION
- ancestral-like phenotype is produced by a different molecular/developmental route.

NOT_IDENTIFIABLE
- available data cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

## Relationship to the Nature headline

This mechanism remains downstream of the inheritance-unit question.

The headline question is whether the evolutionary units of a complex organ are fixed or whether their boundaries can be rewired.

If reconfigurable inheritance is supported, latent-pathway reactivation can explain why certain components are repeatedly available for reassembly.

That would support a stronger synthesis:

Repeated evolution may often draw from a latent developmental repertoire rather than repeatedly inventing phenotype from scratch.

But this sentence is only earned if the retained/suppressed/reactivated pathway sequence is demonstrated.

## Falsifiers

The strict reactivation hypothesis loses if:
- key ancestral pathway genes are deleted or pseudogenized during the loss interval;
- the recurrent state uses a different pathway or different tissue/developmental origin;
- the recurrent state is best explained by introgression from a lineage that never lost the trait;
- no evidence of suppression/persistence exists;
- recurrence is explained by independent recent coding mutations.

## High-impact payoff

The strongest result would not be that a lost colour came back.

It would be:

Complex-organ evolution repeatedly reuses latent ancestral developmental programs, and selection recombines those reactivated programs into new integrated organs.

That would connect evolutionary reversal, developmental constraint, modularity and adaptive radiation in one mechanistic framework.
