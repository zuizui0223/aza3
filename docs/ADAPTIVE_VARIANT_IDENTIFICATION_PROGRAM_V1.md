# aza3 adaptive-variant identification programme v1

**Status:** active mechanistic programme under the Nature-scale master plan  
**Date:** 2026-10-05

## Core question

> Which genomic variants generate rapidly recurring capitulum phenotypes, where did those variants come from, and are the same variants repeatedly reused because they are adaptive?

This programme does **not** define success as finding a candidate gene.

The target chain is:

`genomic variant -> molecular effect -> capitulum phenotype -> ecological performance -> reproductive fitness`

with a second historical chain:

`variant origin -> standing variation / introgression / lineage-specific origin / polyploid-regulatory reuse -> repeated phenotype`

The Nature-scale result requires both chains to meet.

## 1. Evidence ladder

### AV0 — linked biological unit

Same individual or explicitly linked biological unit has:

- standardized phenotype;
- nuclear genotype;
- population identity;
- voucher/diagnostic image;
- cytotype/genome size where relevant.

No gene-level claim is admitted without AV0.

### AV1 — trait-associated genomic region

A locus/haplotype is associated with phenotype after ancestry, population structure and cytotype are controlled.

Allowed claim: **trait-associated genomic region**.

Not yet allowed: causal gene, selected locus, adaptive allele.

### AV2 — molecular mediation

The associated region has a mechanistically concordant molecular effect.

For colour this means, where feasible:

- coding/regulatory haplotype;
- floral-stage RNA expression;
- pigment chemistry;
- calibrated visible/UV phenotype.

Allowed claim: **candidate causal molecular route**.

### AV3 — variant origin

Local genealogy / haplotype age / ancestry distinguishes among:

- lineage-specific recent origin;
- ancestral standing variation;
- introgressive reuse;
- polyploid/homeolog/regulatory reuse;
- unresolved multiple models.

Allowed claim: **source of the phenotypic variant**.

### AV4 — evidence of selection

At least one ancestry-aware selection test supports non-neutral sorting of the focal variant or haplotype.

Examples may include:

- replicated environmental allele-frequency association;
- haplotype-based or site-frequency selection signal where ploidy/model assumptions are valid;
- repeated allele-frequency shift across matched ecological contrasts;
- genotype-specific survival/reproductive selection measured directly in the field.

Selection scans alone do not establish adaptation.

### AV5 — fitness consequence in the predicted environment

The phenotype/allele-associated state changes reproductive performance under a predeclared ecological contrast.

Strongest feasible evidence is a genotype/phenotype-by-environment fitness interaction in ancestry-resolved plants, linked to the proposed mediator.

For the word **adaptive**, AV1 + AV2 + AV5 are mandatory; AV4 strengthens the inference but cannot substitute for fitness.

### AV6 — recurrent reuse

The same old/shared/exchanged genomic solution contributes to the same or homologous phenotype in at least two independently resolved transitions or population histories.

This is the threshold for a **reusable adaptive genomic module** claim.

## 2. Molecular flagship: flower colour

Colour is the first genomic-mechanism target because it has the clearest tractable chain:

`genotype -> anthocyanin/regulatory state -> pigment -> optical phenotype`

and Azami already identifies a robust among-taxon chroma–shortwave-radiation association.

That environmental association is a hypothesis prior only. It is not evidence that anthocyanin alleles are adaptive.

## 3. Focal systems

### C1 — C. japonicum var. takaoense W/BP polymorphism

Current advantage:

- six public transcriptome individuals have recovered morph labels;
- W = 3 and bluish-purple = 3;
- public sample topology already contains morph-associated structure;
- species-tip coding undercounts the known W/C multiplicity.

Current limitation:

- one plant per locality;
- morphology and geography are confounded;
- no population-level causal haplotype or floral expression comparison has been established.

New target:

- 20–30 W and 20–30 BP individuals;
- at least two populations per state;
- mixed populations preferred where they exist;
- otherwise paired nearby populations;
- same-individual flow cytometry, standardized colour, leaf DNA;
- floral RNA and pigment subset.

Primary decision:

> shared ancestry/standing variation, introgressed coloured/white haplotype, or geographically structured independent mechanism?

### C2 — C. brevicaule / C. irumtiense Ryukyu system

Current advantage:

- white versus bluish-purple sister-lineage contrast;
- approximately 0.93 Ma lineage context;
- planned island-population replication;
- strong relevance to the original doctoral colour question.

Current limitation:

- colour direction is not securely polarized;
- species-level topology cannot distinguish ancestral polymorphism, introgression or lineage-specific loss.

New target:

- retain the protected ancestry core: C. brevicaule 60 + C. irumtiense 60;
- 4–5 island populations per taxon where feasible;
- same-individual nuclear ancestry + plastid + cytotype + calibrated colour;
- floral RNA/pigment subset after population ancestry is resolved.

Primary decision:

> homologous molecular switch versus independent regulatory/coding origin, and whether any reused haplotype crossed lineage boundaries.

### C3 — C. pendulum

Role: independent replicated W/coloured system after extant morph/population verification.

Existing species placement is not enough because the public nuclear anchor is continental and not morph-linked.

Protected ancestry target from the existing doctoral gate: 40 individuals.

### C4 — C. sieboldii

Role: independent replicated W/coloured system and bridge to the phyllary programme.

Existing Moreyra material is not a morph-linked Japanese population sample.

Protected ancestry target from the existing doctoral gate: 30 individuals.

## 4. Discovery and validation design

### Stage A — ancestry first

Use population genomic data to define:

- population structure;
- admixture/contact;
- cytotype groups;
- candidate independent phenotype transitions.

Do not perform a pooled colour GWAS across unrelated species and call top hits adaptive genes.

### Stage B — within-system genotype–phenotype mapping

Within each focal system:

- test W/BP or continuous calibrated colour against genotype/haplotype;
- control kinship, population structure and cytotype;
- prioritize replicated associations across populations;
- retain structural variants and regulatory regions where the reference permits.

### Stage C — molecular validation

For the strongest region(s):

- coding haplotype;
- cis/regulatory sequence where recoverable;
- floral RNA at pigmentation onset and fresh anthesis;
- allele/homeolog-specific expression when ploidy permits;
- targeted anthocyanin/pigment chemistry;
- calibrated visible and UV phenotype.

Presence of DFR/ANS or another pathway gene is not sufficient.

### Stage D — origin of the allele

Compare focal local genealogy with genome-wide ancestry.

Classify:

- DE_NOVO_COMPATIBLE;
- STANDING_VARIATION_COMPATIBLE;
- INTROGRESSIVE_REUSE_COMPATIBLE;
- POLYPLOID_REGULATORY_REUSE_COMPATIBLE;
- MULTIPLE_MODELS_REMAIN;
- NOT_IDENTIFIABLE.

### Stage E — adaptation

Test whether plants carrying the focal genotype/phenotype have higher reproductive fitness specifically under the predicted ecological context.

For colour, competing ecological routes remain open:

- pollinator discrimination;
- solar/UV/thermal physiology;
- correlated non-colour functions.

Do not choose the route after genotype results are opened. The ecological contrast must be preregistered for the focal system.

Primary final fitness currency remains mature/filled viable achenes, with plant-level seasonal reproduction where feasible.

### Stage F — repeated reuse

Open the cross-system test only after at least two focal colour systems have AV3-classified variant origins.

Ask whether:

- the same locus is reused;
- different variants in the same regulatory pathway are reused;
- introgressed haplotypes move between lineages;
- entirely independent genetic routes generate the same visible phenotype.

All outcomes are informative.

## 5. What counts as an adaptive gene / variant

Use terminology conservatively:

| Evidence | Allowed term |
|---|---|
| AV1 only | trait-associated locus |
| AV1 + AV2 | candidate causal locus / molecular route |
| AV1 + AV2 + AV4 | selected candidate locus |
| AV1 + AV2 + AV5 | adaptive variant candidate with direct fitness support |
| AV1–AV5 plus strong allele-level causal identification | adaptive variant |
| AV1–AV6 across replicated transitions | reusable adaptive genomic module |

The phrase **adaptive gene** should be avoided when the evidence actually concerns a regulatory haplotype, structural variant, multi-gene block or expression state.

## 6. Nature-scale success condition

The result is not:

> We found an anthocyanin gene associated with white flowers.

The target result is:

> Repeated capitulum phenotypes are generated by old or exchanged genomic variants; at least one such variant has a demonstrated molecular effect and environment-dependent fitness consequence; and the same genomic solution or regulatory module is reused across independent phenotype histories.

This would connect:

`EAzami repeated phenotypic reassembly -> aza3 variant origin -> adaptation -> genomic reuse -> rapid evolvability`

## 7. Falsifiers

The preferred reuse hypothesis weakens if:

- repeated W/C systems map to unrelated recent variants;
- molecular effects differ completely among systems;
- local genealogies track species ancestry with no old/shared/exchanged signal;
- genotype–phenotype association disappears after ancestry control;
- the focal phenotype has no reproductive-fitness effect in the preregistered ecological context.

A strong alternative result would be:

> rapid phenotypic recurrence is produced by many independent molecular routes rather than reuse of a shared genomic toolkit.

That would support genetic redundancy as the source of evolvability instead of genomic reuse.

## 8. Immediate next data priorities

1. Preserve Gate 0A/0B as the independent test of whether phenotype accumulation is genuinely rapid.
2. Protect the existing population-genomic core: brevicaule 60, irumtiense 60, pendulum 40, sieboldii 30.
3. Add a population-replicated takaoense W/BP molecular panel rather than more species-tip sequencing.
4. Use dense resequencing/WGS on the strongest replicated colour systems; RAD remains ancestry/triage only.
5. Collect floral RNA + pigment on the same individual IDs used for ancestry/genotype whenever feasible.
6. Do not call any locus adaptive before the preregistered fitness gate.
