# aza3 Fig.3 causal-claim and novelty gate v1

Status: prospective interpretation guard; **no new biological observation or genome–phenotype association is asserted**.
Date: 2026-10-08.
Applies to: the F / M_global / M_local / R inheritance-unit programme, conditional on C. sieboldii Gate CS.

## Scientific question, not a methodological result

**When a complex reproductive organ remains functionally integrated, what allows evolution to generate and retain different combinations of its components?**

The competing ecological/evolutionary answers are:

- **Stable developmental/genetic modules (conventional modularity):** components segregate among modules but each multi-trait module remains a stable unit. Recombination can create new *between-module* combinations without any change in module boundaries.
- **Reconfigurable genetic architecture:** the effective genomic coupling of traits can cut across current visible modules. Untouched multi-trait phenotypes are predicted better by a fair R than by M_global *and* M_local.
- **Developmental or ecological integration without genomic rewiring:** ontogeny, allometry, plasticity, geographic structure or pollinator-mediated covariance produce integrated phenotypes from largely independent genetic effects.
- **Historical developmental redeployment:** homologous pathways are suppressed and later redeployed in different component combinations. This is a downstream **historical mechanism**, not inferred from sequence orthology or an R-versus-M win.

R is not necessarily the biologically favored answer. Any of these outcomes is a result; observational artifacts are not.

## Strong prior art that rules out easy novelty claims

1. **Bissell & Diggle (2010), Nicotiana:** multi-trait floral modules retained genetic/phenotypic integration after four hybrid generations. Stable genetically inherited floral modules are an explicit alternative to aza3 R. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2010.02040.x
2. **Dellinger et al. (2019), Merianieae:** functional floral modularity and its pattern changed with pollinator shifts, and trait-specific evolutionary rates differed. Thus "floral module boundaries can differ with pollination regimes" is not by itself new. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-019-0697-7
3. **Ho & Smith (2016) and related Iochrominae analyses:** intact pigment-pathway coding sequences after floral pigment loss and possible regain of floral anthocyanins. Conservation/reappearance of floral pigmentation is not a sufficient novelty claim. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-016-0675-3 and https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msy117
4. **Chang et al. (2026), Cirsium japonicum complex:** white and bluish-purple C. japonicum var. takaoense are useful **coexisting taxonomic phenotypes**, but colour-state contrast alone does not establish historical regain, gene reactivation, or head-wide recombination. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6

### What the present Azami–EAzami evidence actually permits

- Azami: predefined groups of visible capitulum constructs show nonrandom phenotypic cohesion. This is *not* evidence that their loci, developmental networks or historical transitions form identical modules.
- Azami ecological fingerprints: no support that the same predefined visible modules share distinct environmental response blocks; climate associations are **not selection estimates**.
- EAzami: observed change lower bounds and asynchronous trait placement under the admissible phylogenies; **not** proven historical module-boundary rewiring.
- Public-reference R1B-0: target/coding-locus and mapping feasibility; **not** evidence of a phenotype-predictive haplotype or recombination.
- Public takaoense Stage A: DFR/ANS sequence-orthology candidates only. The 3 W + 3 BP *young-leaf* RNA-seq screen cannot demonstrate floral reactivation, absence of a pathway, or colour causation.

## Five distinct inferential steps (none may silently stand in for the next)

| Level | Data and discriminating test | Inference ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Visible integration | Same-individual, same-anthesis-stage multiple capitulum traits; reliable phenotypic covariance | Observable phenotypic organization only |
| 1. Present genomic architecture | Fair TRAIN-only F vs M_global vs M_local vs R, untouched cross-population prediction | Present phenotype-predictive genomic architecture; not a historical change of boundaries |
| 2. Component-genomic separability | Independent E2 evidence: phenotype-predictive states in >=2 admitted multi-trait modules, matched LD/MAF/callability windows, ancestry/cytotype and reference controls | A supported mechanistic **genomic-state separation**; not causal molecular regulation |
| 3. Historical reconfiguration or reactivation | Ancestor-informed within-lineage temporal/phylogenetic contrasts of covariance and locus effects; for reactivation, retention -> suppression -> homologous expression -> ideally perturbation | Inferred historical boundary change or a molecular pathway mechanism **only for the specific component** |
| 4. Reproductive consequence | Replicated combinations and mediated pollen deposition/receipt, siring, viable achene set under stage-, habitat-, plant-size-, visitor- and ancestry-aware controls | Functional/fitness impact; adaptation only with selection evidence |

**Important distinction:** an F/M/R result in one segregating population, no matter how predictive, does not identify that the module *boundaries evolved*. A fixed genetic architecture with component-specific loci can produce exactly that statistical result. The strong historical sentence requires Level 3 independently.

## Three decisive ecological contrasts, conditional on the genotype gate

### (i) Natural combinations versus simple standing modularity

Counterfactual: a conventional M model can freely recombine whole modules; it is not forced to predict one floral syndrome. Compare R specifically against **predeclared multi-trait** modules; exclude the single-trait presentation construct from the primary R-versus-M score. Include the phenotype-only TRAIN-derived M_local and ancestry/location-only plus marginal-frequency baselines.

A **rare but correctly predicted** colour x A1–A2 orientation mosaic is compelling only after taxonomic, anthesis, locality and cytotype confounds are excluded. At least three correct mosaic predictions cannot be interpreted as a novel evolutionary configuration if those colour–orientation combinations are commonplace under the baseline.

### (ii) Developmental canalization versus genuine recombination of functional organs

If genomic component scores segregate but adult capitula remain restricted to one narrow phenotypic manifold, that supports developmental/functional **filtering** rather than unlimited reassembly. Conversely, biologically viable, prospectively observed combinations outside the TRAIN manifold would suggest reassembly has an observable consequence. Define the joint phenotype-space metric and sampling-effort controls before unblinding; an E4 prediction from a model is not by itself an observed expansion in evolvability.

### (iii) Functional consequence rather than decorative novelty

Test whether the fitness-relevant response of joint states is additive or interactive. Example for a preregistered orientation × presentation component contrast: do combinations predict measurable pollen placement, wetting/damage mediation, visitation behavior, *and viable achene output* better than a model with additive traits and environmental effects? Trait correlation with precipitation or light is insufficient evidence. Negative or context-dependent interactions are equally informative: reconfigurability may expand variation while selection removes most combinations.

## Explicit claim gates and kill criteria

- **CS not GREEN/LOCAL_SET**: no within-population genomic reassembly claim for C. sieboldii; redirect focal system rather than combining distant or phenologically incomparable records.
- **R beats F but not M**: conventional modularity remains sufficient. No reconfigurability headline.
- **R beats M_global but loses to M_local**: visible global partition likely misspecified locally. No strong headline.
- **R beats M but E2 missing**: classify FIG3_R_PARTIAL, even with >=3 colour-orientation mosaic successes. Never award FIG3_R_STRONG.
- **E2 phenotype-predictive but no historical polarity**: evidence for genomic architectural decoupling, **not for an evolved change in boundary or ancestor's return**.
- **Strong genomic result but no fitness experiment**: no fitness benefit, adaptation, or selection claim. A Nature-scale proposal remains aspirational.
- **White leaf DFR/ANS transcript detected**: evidence of homologous young-leaf transcript, not floral activation; if absent, not proof of genomic pathway loss.

## Scoring implementation safeguard

The programme's original scorer could return `FIG3_R_STRONG` from synthetic or future records using only E1 heldout scores and E3 registered mosaic flags, **without checking E2**. This branch introduces an optional `--e2-receipt` for the independent mechanistic evidence. If it is absent/incomplete, R-over-M can be `FIG3_R_PARTIAL` at most. The receipt must reference an extant SHA256-verified source file, share the frozen prediction-registry hash, and have a frozen-at time before validation phenotype opening.

This receipt is **not self-validating biological proof**: reviewers must inspect the raw genotype-based artifact and its control analyses. The status is an auditable decision gate rather than an estimator of causation. The synthetic CI positive fixture is *only a software test*.

## Next biological action, not more retrospective storytelling

1. **Observe Gate CS:** high-confidence C. sieboldii at A1–A2, colour, continuous orientation and phyllary measurements on the same individuals; never treat post-anthesis head lifting as a heritable upright morph.
2. **If CS passes, phenotype-blind WGS pilot:** determine focal reference transferability, cytotype, relatedness, diversity and power *without* inspecting phenotype-linked locus associations.
3. **Only then** use preregistered multi-trait F/M/R with untouched predictions, E2 matched-window mechanism controls and predeclared functional route. If CS fails, pivot taxa and retain the negative finding.
4. **Historical reactivation as optional separate question:** do not turn a positive orthology or RNA-presence result into a claim about ancestral flower return.

Bottom line: **the compelling unanswered biological question is not whether modularity exists, but whether its inherited constraints actually changed and whether the resulting combinations survive as functioning reproductive organs.**
