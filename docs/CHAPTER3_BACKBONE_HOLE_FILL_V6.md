# Chapter 3 backbone hole-fill sampling v6

## What is being filled

The nationwide species-tree problem is not "collect 120 species x2 from zero" because a compatible public skeleton already exists: Moreyra et al. sampled 38 Japanese Cirsium concepts with the same broad target-capture framework used by EAzami.

Use those public accessions as provisional scaffold tips, then fill three distinct holes:

1. species missing entirely from the 38-tip public skeleton;
2. public tips that do not provide a defensible wild-Japan representative because of identity, cultivated provenance or foreign provenance;
3. otherwise adequate public tips that still lack an own voucher-linked individual carrying Chapter 3 phenotype/cytotype measurements.

Exact branch placement of a newly sampled species is **not preassigned**. The sample fills a coverage slot; its branch is inferred by target capture.

## Nominal 120-species arithmetic

If the reconciled Japanese operational census is 120 species:

- 38 species/concepts already have Moreyra public representatives;
- about 82 species are absent from that public skeleton;
- 9 of the 38 current public tips require high-priority wild-Japan/identity repair;
- the remaining 29 can provisionally contribute one public representative.

### H1 — empty species slots

82 absent species x2 new wild individuals = **164 new individuals**.

These are the largest tree holes. Take two independent wild Japanese individuals, preferably from two populations or separated occurrences. The two are not required to come from opposite ends of a species range unless the taxon is widespread.

### H2 — broken/weak existing tips

Current high-priority repair set:

- identity blocked: JPN29 `C. verutum`, JPN31 `C. yuki-uenoanum`, JPN33 `C. effusum`;
- cultivated/foreign provenance requiring wild-Japan repair: JPN32 `C. buergeri`, JPN34 `C. microspicatum`, JPN35 `C. nipponicum`, JPN36 `C. sieboldii`, JPN37 `C. kamtschaticum`, JPN38 `C. pendulum`.

9 concepts x2 new wild individuals = **18 new individuals**.

The old public accession is not deleted. It is retained as a labelled sensitivity accession where taxonomically meaningful, but it does not occupy one of the two primary wild-Japan slots.

### H3 — one-public-tip plus one own individual

For the remaining nominal 29 adequate public tips, keep the public target-capture accession and collect **one** new wild Japanese voucher-linked individual.

29 x1 = **29 new individuals**.

The new individual carries the Chapter 3 trait package, so the species becomes a public+own two-representative unit without needlessly collecting two new tree-only plants.

### Result

Phase A: H1 + H2 = **182 new individuals**. This first fills empty species tips and repairs broken tips.

Phase B: H3 = **+29 new individuals**.

Total new material needed for a nominal 120-species, two-representative primary matrix is therefore **211 new individuals**, not 240 new individuals.

The primary matrix then consists of:

- 211 new own individuals;
- 29 retained adequate Moreyra public representatives;
- total = **240 primary representatives = 2 per species**.

General formula:

`new = 2*(N-38) + (38-R) + 2R = 2N - 38 + R`

where `N` is the final admitted Japanese species count and `R` is the number of Moreyra concepts requiring two-new-sample repair. With `N=120, R=9`, new = **211**.

## Where to put the first new samples

"Where" means a missing lineage/coverage slot, not an assumed branch.

### G1 — isolated island lineages

Fill before routine mainland duplicates because omission can leave major lineage space invisible.

Verified examples include:

- `C. boninense` — Ogasawara;
- `C. hachijoense` — Izu Islands;
- `C. sadoense` — Sado;
- `C. brevicaule` — central Ryukyus;
- `C. irumtiense` — southern Ryukyus;
- `C. umezawanum` — Rishiri.

Sampling placement:

- multi-island species: use two different islands when feasible;
- single-island endemic: use two separated localities;
- known hybrid contact: place baseline representatives outside the obvious contact zone, then treat contact-zone material as later network enrichment.

### G2 — Hokkaido/northern gap

The 38-tip scaffold contains only a small fraction of the northern radiation. Early missing-tip candidates include `C. apoense`, `C. yezoalpinum`, `C. pectinellum`, `C. albrechtii`, `C. boreale`, and `C. iito-kojianum`.

Two representatives/species fill species-tree tips. Do not turn them into 10–15-individual population projects before Level 2 asks for it.

### G3 — alpine/narrow mainland gaps

Examples: `C. zawoense`, `C. ugoense`, `C. fauriei`, `C. babanum`, `C. furusei`.

These have high hole-filling value because a sparse backbone can otherwise turn a cluster of short-range alpine taxa into one artificially long unsampled interval.

### G4 — western regional radiations

Examples include `C. calcicola`, `C. ashiuense`, `C. taishakuense`, `C. kirishimense`, `C. nishimeraense`, `C. austrokiusianum`.

Again, two tree representatives first. Population depth comes later.

The example lists are **not the final census**. The 161 authority records still have to be reconciled into the operational species list before the H1 count is frozen.

## How focal RAD fits into the holes

Do not collect separate tree-only plants when a focal population bank already supplies better representatives.

- `C. sieboldii`: H2 wild-Japan repair samples come from its RAD bank.
- `C. dipsacolepis`: the H3 own trait-linked tree sample comes from the RAD bank.
- `C. lineare`: same.
- `C. brevicaule`: the two H1 tree representatives are one individual from Okinawa Honto and one from Amami Oshima.
- `C. irumtiense`: the two H1 tree representatives are one from Miyako and one from Ishigaki.

Thus target capture is a **selection of individuals from the biological bank**, not a second collecting programme.

## Acquisition priority

1. **H1 empty species tip** — two samples.
2. **H2 broken Moreyra tip** — two samples.
3. **H3 adequate public tip lacking own trait-linked sample** — one sample.
4. **focal RAD top-up beyond the tree representatives** — only where P01/P02/M01 already requires it.
5. **H4 third/fourth target-capture individual** — only after the first nationwide tree/network identifies non-monophyly, strong discordance, ploidy/hybrid problems or major geographic structure.

This is the hole-fill rule: **fill an empty or broken species slot before adding redundant depth to a tip that is already represented.**
