# aza3 Nature-scale master plan v1

Status: active strategic plan
Date: 2026-10-04

## Core question

Why could a young thistle radiation repeatedly generate so much capitulum diversity in so little evolutionary time?

The high-risk working hypothesis is that rapid capitulum diversification was enabled by recurrent reuse, sorting, exchange and regulatory redeployment of pre-existing genomic variation, allowing different capitulum components to be recombined rather than reinvented de novo.

This is a hypothesis, not an EAzami result.

## 1. Direct bridge from EAzami to aza3

EAzami result 1:
36/38 sampled Japanese concepts occur in one dominant young radiation.

aza3 hypothesis H0:
Phenotypic innovation accumulated unusually rapidly within that radiation.

EAzami result 2:
Orientation requires 4–6 minimum changes, phyllary posture 3 and stickiness 5.

aza3 hypothesis H1:
Repeated phenotypic states may repeatedly draw on reusable genetic variation rather than requiring a new de novo solution every time.

EAzami result 3:
The three traits occupy unequal evolutionary depths and 0/3 trait pairs pass robust shared transition localization.

aza3 hypothesis H2:
Different capitulum components may have partly separable genomic histories that can be recombined into new head configurations.

EAzami result 4:
Species-tip coding loses state multiplicity in 4/4 audited polymorphic systems.

aza3 hypothesis H3:
The relevant evolutionary substrate may still segregate within and among populations, so species-tip genomes are insufficient.

EAzami result 5:
No recurring coarse climate or sea-level trigger was identified.

aza3 design consequence:
Do not make another broad climate search the main route. Ask what made repeated change possible.

The genomic-reuse hypothesis is generated by these results but is not demonstrated by them.

## 2. Gate 0 — first prove that the phenomenon is genuinely rapid

Before using the phrase exceptional evolvability, quantify phenotype accumulation against evolutionary time.

### Gate 0A — obtain a defensible time denominator first

The current Japan38 scaffold is an undated substitutions/site phylogram. The exact machine-readable Moreyra et al. (2025) RelTime chronogram has not been recovered in the existing public audit. Therefore no transition-per-Myr or disparity-through-time analysis is authorized from the current scaffold alone.

The recovery order is frozen in `GATE0A_DATED_TREE_READINESS_AND_RECOVERY_V1.md` and `aza3_gate0a_recovery_routes_v1.csv`:

1. recover the original author/source dated-tree bundle;
2. if unavailable, independently rebuild and validate a dated ensemble from public sequence data under frozen calibration rules;
3. if neither succeeds, retain local dated East-Asian context only and leave the Japan-wide rate premise unresolved.

Only a recovered or independently validated dated ensemble opens Gate 0B.

### Gate 0B — test phenotype accumulation rate

Gate 0B is currently **not authorized** because exact Japanese continuous-trait coverage is still sparse even though the global Azami phenotype atlas is broad.

Current strict-spatial overlap:
- 14 exact Japan38 concepts with Azami trait matches;
- 17 concepts represented only at binomial level;
- 6 distinct Japan38 trait taxa with at least 10 observations;
- 18 of 36 Japan38 species binomials absent from the current exhaustive Azami source pool.

The primary future test is frozen in `GATE0B_PHENOTYPE_RATE_READINESS_V1.md` and `aza3_gate0b_rate_estimand_v1.json`.

Primary core axes:
- presentation angle;
- floral lightness;
- floral chroma;
- head elongation;
- head compactness.

Primary estimand:

`R_total = sum_m sigma2_J,m / sum_m sigma2_BG,m`

where sigma2_J,m is the dated evolutionary-rate estimate for core axis m in the dominant Japanese radiation and sigma2_BG,m is the corresponding non-Japanese *Cirsium* background rate on the same dated tree and standardized scale.

The null is `R_total = 1`. Calibration must use parametric simulation on the actual dated Japanese subtree while propagating coverage, missingness, measurement error and dated-tree uncertainty.

Before trait-rate outcomes are opened, an outcome-blind precision simulation determines whether the recovered Japanese coverage is sufficient to identify `R_total`. If not, the result is `COVERAGE_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE` and trait recovery continues.

EAzami orientation, phyllary posture and stickiness remain an independent secondary layer for time-normalized transition summaries once a dated ensemble exists. They do not substitute for the primary global continuous comparator because homologous global state coding is unavailable for all three modules.

Falsifier:
If the dominant radiation is not unusually fast after age, topology, coverage and sampling controls, the Nature-scale accelerated-evolvability claim is weakened. The project can still study the genomic origin of repeated traits, but not exceptional innovation rate.

## 3. Four competing genomic source models

### G1 — repeated de novo mutation
Each repeated phenotype is produced by a new lineage-specific mutation or regulatory solution.

Predictions:
- repeated phenotype events use different causal variants;
- local genealogies mostly follow background ancestry;
- relevant variants are compatible with recent lineage-specific origin.

### G2 — ancestral standing variation
Trait-associated variants predate focal lineage splits and are repeatedly sorted among descendants.

Predictions:
- old homologous haplotypes recur in separated lineages with the same state;
- trait-associated variant age predates focal divergence;
- phenotype sharing can occur without recent gene flow.

### G3 — introgressive reuse
Trait-associated haplotypes move between lineages after divergence.

Predictions:
- phenotype-associated regions have local genealogies discordant with genome-wide ancestry;
- explicit gene-flow/local-ancestry tests support exchange involving state-sharing populations;
- shared phenotype blocks are more similar than expected from genomic background.

### G4 — polyploid, homeolog or regulatory reuse
Existing toolkits generate alternate states through dosage, homeolog sorting or regulatory reweighting.

Predictions:
- phenotype associates with cytotype, dosage, allele-specific or homeolog-specific expression;
- the core biochemical/developmental toolkit remains present across states;
- state changes need not require new coding-sequence innovation.

Different capitulum modules may have different winning models.

## 4. Sampling strategy — sample evolutionary decisions, not species lists

Every individual should link:
- immutable individual ID;
- taxon concept;
- population ID;
- deidentified locality;
- voucher or diagnostic image;
- direct capitulum phenotype;
- nuclear genomic sample;
- cytotype/genome-size measurement or explicit missingness;
- plastid companion where informative;
- authorization record.

Priority systems:

A. Repeated or polymorphic colour systems
Role: molecular flagship.
Question: shared causal loci, standing variation, introgression or regulatory reuse?
Follow-up: floral expression and pigment chemistry.

B. Repeated orientation systems
Role: ecological flagship.
Question: genomic source of repeated orientation states plus separate orientation × wetting fitness test.

C. JPN06–JPN15 and replicated sticky/nonsticky systems
Question: old/shared/introgressed surface-trait variants versus independent origins.

D. JPN36 phyllary system and ancestry-matched relatives
Question: genomic source of a strongly localized phyllary change.

E. Polymorphic populations and contact zones
Highest information value because they preserve segregating variation and allow direct tests of ancestry–phenotype linkage and recombination.

### Active Phase-A sampling panel

The active population-genomic discovery panel is frozen in `AZA3_NATURE_GENOMIC_SAMPLING_PANEL_V1.md` and `aza3_nature_genomic_sampling_panel_v1.csv`.

It retains the previously recommended **298 individuals** but changes their inferential role from generic population history to discrimination among the four genomic-source models.

Phase-A systems:
- *C. pendulum* — 60;
- *C. sieboldii* — 40;
- *C. lineare* — 24;
- *C. dipsacolepis* — 24;
- *C. brevicaule* — 75;
- *C. irumtiense* — 75.

*C. sieboldii* is the primary same-species test of genomic combinatorial reuse because current source evidence indicates colour polymorphism plus variation/relevance across orientation, phyllary and stickiness modules. These states must be remeasured on the sequenced individuals; species-level authority states cannot substitute for individual phenotype.

The six morph-labelled public *C. japonicum* var. *takaoense* transcriptomes are retained as a Phase-B molecular anchor, not population replication.

## 5. Genomic data tiers

Tier 1 — ancestry and triage
Retain the original common RAD or comparable population-genomic panel for:
- population structure;
- admixture;
- same-individual phenotype–ancestry linkage;
- cytotype-aware ancestry;
- selection of focal contrasts for deep sequencing.

Claim ceiling:
Tier-1 reduced-representation data alone are not enough for fine-scale haplotype age, regulatory mechanism or structural-variant reuse.

Tier 2 — dense resequencing
Use whole-genome resequencing or an equivalently dense design on the strongest replicated transitions to test:
- haplotype sharing;
- local genealogy/topology weighting;
- introgression/local ancestry;
- relative allele age;
- phenotype association;
- genomic-block reuse.

Ploidy-aware analysis is mandatory.

Tier 3 — mechanism-specific molecular follow-up
Opened only for interpretable focal systems:
- floral RNA-seq;
- allele/homeolog-specific expression;
- pigment chemistry;
- candidate regulatory-region sequencing;
- structural-variant validation;
- targeted long-read/reference improvement if necessary.

## 6. The Nature kill shot — genomic combinatorial reuse

The strongest result is not merely that standing variation exists.

The decisive pattern would be:

1. repeated phenotypic states;
2. different capitulum components associated with different genomic regions or haplotypes;
3. evidence that at least some of those variants predate or cross lineage boundaries;
4. different capitulum components carrying different local ancestry histories in the same individuals or populations;
5. novel head configurations arising from new combinations of those histories.

That would translate the EAzami result

phenotypic components change separately

into the aza3 mechanism

genomic components can be inherited, exchanged and sorted separately.

This is the direct EAzami → aza3 hypothesis bridge.

## 7. Functional and fitness tests are representative, not exhaustive

Orientation:
gravity-referenced orientation → wetting/presentation → pollen or stigma process → filled viable achenes.
Primary ecological test: orientation × rain/wetting.

Colour:
genotype/regulation → pigment → optical phenotype.
Then discriminate pollinator signalling versus radiation/thermal/UV function.

Phyllary:
access manipulation → antagonist access/damage → legitimate visitor process → viable seed.

Stickiness:
neutralization → arthropod-guild access/community → pollination/antagonism → viable seed.

The Nature package does not require every trait to have a complete molecular and ecological chain. One or two traits can provide deep causal exemplars while broader traits test genomic reuse.

## 8. How the four genomic models are classified

For each repeated phenotype event, end with one of:

- DE_NOVO_COMPATIBLE
- STANDING_VARIATION_COMPATIBLE
- INTROGRESSIVE_REUSE_COMPATIBLE
- POLYPLOID_REGULATORY_REUSE_COMPATIBLE
- MULTIPLE_MODELS_REMAIN
- NOT_IDENTIFIABLE

Do not force all events into one source class.

## 9. Generality

Minimum:
replicate the genomic-source test across more than one capitulum module and more than one transition/polymorphic system within Japan.

Stronger:
freeze predictions from Japan and test an independent Cirsium radiation or major lineage, ideally the North American rapid radiation if homologous data can be obtained.

## 10. Success levels

Strong doctoral result:
- population-level data recover recent transitions hidden at species-tip level;
- at least one repeated trait is convincingly assigned to standing variation, introgression or regulatory reuse;
- one focal trait has a closed function/fitness chain.

Strong high-tier result:
- multiple transitions reuse pre-existing genomic variation;
- at least two phenotype modules have separable genomic histories;
- genomic reuse explains phenotype combinations better than repeated de novo origin;
- representative fitness evidence shows biological consequence.

Nature-scale result:
1. capitulum diversity accumulated unusually rapidly;
2. repeated states frequently use old/shared/exchanged genomic variation;
3. different capitulum modules have partly independent genomic histories;
4. recombination or sorting generates novel phenotype combinations;
5. the principle predicts an independent radiation or replicated module set;
6. representative phenotype–function–fitness chains link genomic reuse to ecology.

Possible headline:
Recombination and reuse of ancestral genomic modules fuel rapid innovation of a complex reproductive organ.

## 11. What would kill the preferred hypothesis

The reusable-toolkit hypothesis loses strength if:
- Gate 0 finds no accelerated phenotype accumulation;
- repeated phenotype events use unrelated recent variants;
- phenotype-associated local genealogies always follow genome-wide ancestry;
- different capitulum modules share one inseparable genomic history;
- admixture or standing variation does not predict phenotype combinations;
- independent replication fails.

A failure of genomic reuse can still produce a strong alternative conclusion: rapid phenotypic innovation may arise through high genetic redundancy and many independent molecular routes.

## 12. Immediate execution order

1. Gate 0: quantify whether capitulum innovation is genuinely rapid.
2. Freeze population sampling around replicated phenotype-history discriminators.
3. Run Tier-1 same-individual ancestry + phenotype + cytotype panel.
4. Select focal replicated transitions before deep sequencing.
5. Run Tier-2 dense resequencing to discriminate the four genomic source models.
6. Use colour as the molecular flagship.
7. Use orientation as the ecological causal flagship.
8. Test genomic combinatorial reuse across modules.
9. Replicate/generalize in an independent lineage if feasible.

## Stop rules

- Do not call the radiation exceptionally evolvable before Gate 0.
- Do not infer standing variation simply because a variant occurs in multiple taxa.
- Do not infer introgression from one discordant tree.
- Do not infer causal loci from ancestry-correlated association alone.
- Do not infer genomic modularity from EAzami phenotypic mosaicism.
- Do not treat ploidy as causal without same-individual cytotype/genomic evidence.
- Do not use RAD alone for fine-scale haplotype-origin claims.
- Do not require every capitulum trait to have the same genomic source.
- Do not call repeated phenotypes adaptive without representative fitness evidence.
- Do not add analyses that cannot discriminate the four genomic models or test Gate 0.
