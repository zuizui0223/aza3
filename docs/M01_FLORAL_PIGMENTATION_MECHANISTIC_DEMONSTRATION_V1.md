# M01 — floral pigmentation mechanistic demonstration v1

Status date: 2026-09-01

## Why M01 exists

The core Chapter 3 programme asks where trait-state changes remain identifiable once public species-tip histories are replaced by own ancestry data linked to the same individuals. M01 extends that logic one layer downward: after a trait contrast is defined, can the contrast be decomposed into historical direction, biochemical state, molecular regulation and selection-consistent genomic evidence?

M01 is an independent mechanistic demonstration, not P06. It is not derived from the locked Chapter 2 P01-P05 priority ranking and cannot reorder or replace that ranking. Failure, ambiguity or cancellation of M01 leaves the core history-discrimination programme intact.

The worked example is floral pigmentation in `Cirsium brevicaule` and `Cirsium irumtiense`. These taxa are useful because they provide a conspicuous white versus bluish-purple corolla contrast that can be followed from phenotype to pigment and gene expression. The dissertation question is not “why are these two species different?” in isolation. The pair is used to demonstrate what a trait transition means when followed below the topology level.

## External premise, not focal result

Chang et al. (2026, BMC Plant Biology 26:545; DOI `10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6`) is admitted only as a reference premise. It supports treating `C. brevicaule` and `C. irumtiense` as distinct Ryukyu lineages/species separated across the Miyako Strait, reports white versus bluish-purple corollas, and reports a strong 2C genome-size difference of approximately 1.93 pg versus 0.69 pg.

M01 therefore does not make basic species delimitation its primary estimand and does not treat a few Sanger loci such as matK, rbcL, trnL-F and ITS as a replacement for that phylogenomic backbone. The published result does not establish ancestral floral colour, loss versus gain, the molecular basis of the contrast, selection on a candidate mechanism or the identity of a selective agent.

## Primary question

> Can the floral-pigmentation contrast be decomposed into ancestral-state history, pigment chemistry, same-individual corolla expression and genomic evidence without presupposing regain, loss or a selective agent?

The words `regain`, `re-evolution` and `reactivation` are output-dependent terms. They are prohibited at the starting state.

## Frozen competing histories

### H1 — ancestral pigment followed by loss or reduction

The relevant ancestor was pigmented and the `C. brevicaule` side subsequently lost or strongly reduced floral anthocyanin pigmentation.

Support requires a topology-robust ancestral-state result that favours a pigmented ancestor and localizes the loss/reduction to the `C. brevicaule` side. A white present-day flower alone is not sufficient.

### H2 — ancestral unpigmented state followed by secondary gain or regain

The relevant ancestor was unpigmented and the `C. irumtiense` side subsequently gained or regained floral anthocyanin pigmentation.

Support requires a topology-robust ancestral-state result that favours an unpigmented ancestor and localizes gain to the `C. irumtiense` side. Differential expression by itself cannot establish that the pathway was historically lost and later restored.

### H3 — simple H1/H2 history not identified

Ancestral state, transition placement, pigment chemistry or reticulation remains ambiguous, or the data indicate multiple chemically or phylogenetically distinct changes. H3 is a valid scientific outcome, not a failed version of H1 or H2.

H1, H2 and H3 are all `COMPETING_NOT_PREFERRED` before focal data are admitted.

## Evidence ladder

### E0 — phenotype only

Evidence: visible or spectrally measured white versus bluish-purple contrast, with ancestral state unresolved.

Maximum claim: the contrast exists. No loss, regain, re-evolution or molecular-mechanism claim.

### E1 — history plus pigment chemistry

Evidence: an admitted topology ensemble, appropriately sampled related taxa, topology-robust ancestral-state inference and homologous pigment chemistry.

Maximum claim: loss, secondary gain or unresolved history may be identified. No molecular mechanism or selection claim.

### E2 — same-individual molecular association

Evidence: E1 plus same-individual corolla pigment chemistry and biologically replicated, developmentally matched corolla RNA-seq or independently validated expression for anthocyanin structural and regulatory genes.

Candidate pathways include structural genes such as CHS, CHI, F3H, F3'H/F3'5'H, DFR, ANS and glycosylation/transport components, together with MYB, bHLH and WD40 regulatory components. These are candidate classes, not pre-declared causal genes.

Maximum claim: a molecular-expression mechanism is associated with the transition. Expression difference alone is not a causal regulatory variant, adaptation or selection result.

### E3 — selection-consistent genomic evidence

Evidence: E2 plus explicit orthology/callability qualification, population replication within each focal lineage, a frozen neutral demographic/background-structure model and concordant evidence from multiple nonredundant selection statistics at a phenotype/expression-linked region.

The reported genome-size difference creates a specific mapping-bias gate. Cross-lineage analyses must show that orthology, reference choice, callable regions and mapping success do not manufacture the focal signal. If this cannot be qualified, the cross-lineage selection lane stops.

A two-species FST outlier alone, branch-specific dN/dS alone, one sweep statistic alone, or a threshold selected after candidate inspection is insufficient. Absence of a detectable sweep is not evidence that historical selection did not occur.

Maximum claim: the candidate region shows selection-consistent evidence beyond the frozen demographic/background model. This is not proof of adaptation, and genomics alone cannot identify the selective agent.

### E4 — separate causal validation

Evidence: E3 plus a separately authorized, preregistered direct perturbation and fitness design.

Maximum claim: only the molecular or fitness effect directly estimated by that experiment. Pollinator, antagonist, climate or another agent can be named causally only if an agent-specific ecological design directly supports that attribution.

## Sampling that M01 actually requires

M01 E0-E3 does not require manipulative pollination experiments, bagging, single-visit assays or long camera deployments. The minimal field contribution can be limited to authorized collection of linked materials.

For every admitted individual, retain the same immutable ID across:

- accepted taxon concept and population ID;
- deidentified locality key;
- voucher or voucher-linked diagnostic images;
- standardized corolla colour or spectral phenotype;
- pigment-chemistry material when authorized;
- developmentally matched corolla RNA material when authorized;
- leaf DNA material and genomic library ID when authorized;
- authorization and conservation-review IDs.

For E3, population replication is mandatory. A comparison of one `C. brevicaule` sample set against one `C. irumtiense` sample set is a lineage contrast, not a selection test. Neutral structure and demographic history are estimated before focal candidate interpretation.

## Selection and selective-agent boundary

Selection is treated as a separate inference layer from mechanism. Candidate regions should either be nominated prospectively from the pigment/expression result or tested by a genome-wide procedure with frozen multiplicity control. Multiple nonredundant statistics and phenotype/expression concordance are required.

Even a strong E3 result does not show that butterflies, flies, bees, climate or another factor caused the selection. Genomics alone cannot identify the selective agent. Pollinators remain candidate ecological agents for a later, separately designed test; they are not required for M01 to succeed through E3.

## Stop rules

- unresolved ancestral state across the admitted topology and trait ensemble -> remain at E0 and do not use loss/regain language;
- pigment chemistry not homologously comparable -> do not reduce the visual contrast to one anthocyanin transition;
- corolla RNA not developmentally matched or biologically replicated -> no E2 mechanism claim;
- orthology, callable regions or mapping bias not qualified -> no cross-lineage E3 interpretation;
- no within-lineage population replication or demographic background model -> no E3 selection claim;
- genomic evidence only -> no causal pollinator or other selective-agent claim;
- M01 failure or ambiguity -> no effect on P01-P05 validity or ordering.
