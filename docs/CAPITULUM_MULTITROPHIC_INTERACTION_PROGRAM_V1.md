# Capitulum as a multitrophic access interface — hypothesis and causal evidence plan v1

**Status:** 2026-10-08 prospective programme. No focal Japanese Cirsium parasitoid-access, orientation × true-spine manipulation, trait-mediated fitness, or adaptive evolution result is asserted.

## Biological question

**Can the same orientation and involucre/spine geometry channel legitimate pollination, damaging seed-predator oviposition, and the enemies of those seed predators in different directions or at different times?**

This differs from the already-known display/attraction–seed-predation trade-off. It asks whether apparent defence creates *selective ecological corridors* and whether a trait can, unexpectedly, **shield a seed predator from its own parasitoids**.

The organ is not treated as an automatic unitary reproductive strategy: small florets, immature achenes, phyllaries, sticky exudates and floral presentation may mediate distinct interaction routes.

## Empirical starting points and limits

1. **Natural thistle multitrophic precedent.** Walker, Hartley & Jones (2008), *Cirsium arvense*–seed-feeding tephritids–Hymenoptera parasitoids, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2656.2008.01406.x. Bud resources and plant conditions affect tephritid abundance, while parasitoid pressure also varies; larvae can develop within heads. This establishes a plant–herbivore–parasitoid system, not mediation by floral orientation or true spines.
2. **Host and parasitoid use the same capitulum.** Vanbergen et al. (2006), *C. palustre*–Tephritidae–parasitoids, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2656.2006.01099.x. Adult flies use recently opened heads for oviposition and parasitoids probe heads to attack larvae; the physical route and stage of interaction are relevant. No spine × angle fitness effects are identified.
3. **Head entry has reproductive consequences.** Gijsman et al. (2020), *C. pitcheri*–*Larinus planus*, Global Ecology and Conservation 22 e00945. Oviposition scars on phyllaries indicate entry/egg laying at the head surface; infested heads produced about 60% fewer mature seeds. This does not establish antagonism by parasitoids or orientation.
4. **Selective spine effect exists in related Cardueae.** Agrawal et al. (2000), *Centaurea solstitialis*, DOI 10.2307/3672545: removing involucral spines lowered filled seed percentage by 22%; legitimate bee/fly visit probability did not increase, while their per-visit time increased; illegitimate Lepidoptera were deterred. No orientation test.
5. **Same thistle display attracts and exposes.** Ohashi & Yahara (1998, 2000), *C. purpuratum*: bee use responds to plant display while pre-dispersal seed predation also increases with floral production. DOIs 10.2307/2446309 and 10.1139/b99-182. Plant-level head number and head-level barrier effects must remain distinct.
6. **Sticky defence is not universal.** Thomas (2003), *C. discolor*, DOI 10.22543/0090-0222.2085: neutralizing sticky exudates did not produce the predicted increases in seed predators or decreases in seed production in that experiment. Do not promote an assumed predator-attraction / trap pathway.
7. **Existing repositories:** Azami includes 2-D image orientation and projection geometry (not biological spine length), EAzami documents asynchronous orientation/phyllary/stickiness histories plus large reproductive-herbivory costs, BITA documents consumer- and context-dependent defence selectivity, and EAzami's V10-to-aza3 causal handoff already asks that parasitoids, ants, predators, pollinators and antagonists be scored as separate guilds. These are mechanistic priors, not results of the proposed 3-way head interaction.

## Organism roles are actions, not taxa

For each head and observation window record:
- **Effective mutualist:** anther/stigma pollen transfer or reproductive contact, not any insect landing.
- **Floral exploiter:** nectar thieving/robbing or direct floral damage; distinguish damage from legitimate foraging, including on Lepidoptera.
- **Seed antagonist:** adult ovipositor/egg scar -> established larva -> quantified floret/receptacle/achene damage. A fly adult on a flower is not automatically a seed predator.
- **Antagonist enemy:** parasitoid searching/probing, actual parasitism (where authorized rearing/dissection shows it), predatory arthropod consumption of pest. An observed parasitoid is not proven to save plant seeds.
- **Alternative consumers/mediators:** ants, aphids, spiders and other arthropods, recorded with guild interactions rather than automatically classified beneficial or detrimental.

Separate the time axis: bud/pre-flowering oviposition, pollen-presentation/anthesis, internal larval feeding/parasitoid access, and seed maturation. The actual access interval may differ among species.

## Mechanistic predictions

### H1 selective barrier ("guarded pollination")
An O × S combination decreases **successful seed-antagonist oviposition per approach**, with little or no decline in **effective pollen transfer per legitimate pollinator approach**. The benefit reaches viable achenes in the same focal heads.

### H2 enemy-shielding reversal (leading surprising hypothesis)
Spines/phyllaries can protect **internal seed antagonists** if they obstruct parasitoid ovipositors more than earlier antagonist egg-laying routes. The result can be *more surviving seed-feeding larvae and lower plant fitness under more seemingly defensive anatomy*. An even stronger prediction is that the sign of the apparent spine benefit changes with the density and timing of natural enemy pressure.

**Timing condition:** killing a parasitoid's host AFTER most achene damage is complete does not provide a seed-rescue benefit. The main object is parasitoid-caused mortality **before** the relevant damage, not indiscriminate parasitism of larvae.

### H3 guild / coordinate-frame switching
Orientation rotates spine locations relative to gravity, light/wind and insect approach directions, but **does not** change the spine–floret geometry in head-centred coordinates. Thus:
- If insect access is purely head-centred, changing O while holding S fixed has little reason to alter spine-dependent entry efficiency.
- If flying pollinators, lateral ovipositors, stem crawlers and external parasitoids use different world-centred access approaches, the sign of `O × S` effects can diverge by guild.
A camera-derived *world azimuth/elevation of approach relative to vertical* and its *head-centred transformed direction* are required to distinguish these.

### H4 temporal/ecological reversal
A barrier that prevents initial seed-antagonist oviposition may later block parasitoid attacks. If head orientation or phyllary accessibility changes between anthesis and fruit maturation, a phenotype's apparent defensive value may reverse through time. A fruit-stage erect head that was nodding at anthesis is **ontogenetic reorientation**, not an independent inherited orientation morph.

### H5 evolutionary constraint, downstream only
The natural adult O–S combination or genetic covariance may be weak even when strong, contrasting environment-specific joint selection occurs. Positive guild-specific function does not prove correlated evolutionary origin. Require independently segregating variation or robust historical genetic/phylogenetic evidence before `coevolution`, `adaptation of the combination` or `module-boundary change` language.

## Minimal measurable causal graph

```text
O (gravity-referenced head angle) + S (botanically measured involucre/spine geometry)
  -> 3-D access route by guild and time
     -> legitimate pollen deposition / siring
     -> seed-predator adult oviposition -> damaging larvae
     -> parasitoid attack -> predator/parasitoid-induced EARLY larval mortality
  -> filled / viable achenes (plant-level reproductive fitness, scaled appropriately)
```

A **conditional explanatory approximation**, only when early parasitism reduces seed feeding, is

```text
Expected larval damage ~ eggs established(O,S) × [1 - effective_pre-damage_parasitism(O,S)] × damage_per_surviving_larva.
```

Under that explicit timing assumption, stronger barriers can reduce initial oviposition yet increase *net* damage if they disproportionately exclude early parasitoids. This is a sign-indefinite ecological mechanism, not a fitted law.

Additional paths, to be tested instead of dismissed: `O -> wetting/UV/thermal timing -> pollen viability`, `S -> pollen landing interference`, floral density/displays, ontogenetic timing, species/ancestry and geography.

## Measurements and identifiability: three phases, without presuming approval

**Phase A: observation-only access ecology (first gate).** On at least several plants per candidate population with independent head IDs: standardized head angle at A1–A2, repeated same-head pre/post-anthesis orientation, exact spine length/direction/stiffness or non-invasive validated access-gap measures, total floret number and head diameter, environmental/phenological context. Observe insect arrivals, *route* (flies to stigma, bract probing, stem crawling, parasitoid searching), legitimate pollen contact and oviposition evidence in matched camera effort across head types. Do not infer absent parasitoids from low sample counts.

**Phase B: fate linkage.** Follow the *same head* to later visible damage, oviposition scar counts, where authorized dissection/emergence/rearing identifies seed feeders and parasitoids, and total/filled/viable achenes. Parasitoid activity without evidence of predamage feeding suppression can never establish enemy shielding. Repeated visitor bouts are not independent plant replicates.

**Phase C: causal factorial, only if safe and authorized.** Randomize orientation vs sham and validated *reversible external access geometry* vs sham with independent treatment verification. This first estimates current `O × S` effects on guild entry and viable achenes, not direct change in actual spine length. If both antagonist and parasitoid exposure stages are observed, a separately qualified enemy-access contrast may be needed to isolate the parasitoid pathway. Broad insecticide treatment or cages that also exclude pollinators cannot be interpreted as parasitoid-specific. A failed device safety/independent manipulation gate stops the causal claim. Existing JPN36 n=24 protocol is **only a two-arm feasibility pilot** and is not an authorized full factorial.

## Quantitative estimands: do not equate incompatible outcomes

For a declared response Y measured on the same valid scale:
`Delta_OS(Y) = E[Y|do(O=1,S=1)] - E[Y|do(O=1,S=0)] - E[Y|do(O=0,S=1)] + E[Y|do(O=0,S=0)]`.

Compute **separate** `Delta_OS` outcomes for pollen deposition/seed set, herbivore successful entry, parasitoid successful early attack, and final viable achenes. These are *not* four pieces of an automatically identified mediation decomposition.

Potential preregistered contrast for enemy-shielding: conditional on **independently ascertained prior infestation and parasitoid exposure**, does the access-geometry perturbation reduce effective parasitoid attack / increase damaging-larva survival? A convincing plant-fitness paradox additionally requires fewer viable achenes despite reduced new oviposition, without missingness/selection-bias due to choosing post-treatment infested heads. Prefer prospective infestation strata before treatment; do not condition the primary total effect on post-treatment damage.

Independent heldout sites/years are necessary before claiming transportability; small exploratory guild cells are `NOT_IDENTIFIABLE`.

## Strong alternative outcomes and falsification

- Orientation × spine affects only **rain/pollen wetting**, not ecological access: biotic mechanism rejected, abiotic hypothesis remains viable.
- Feeding larvae are inaccessible to parasitoids regardless of S: enemy-shielding rejected for that guild.
- Parasitoids attack only after irrecoverable seed damage: parasitoid influence cannot be assumed to rescue seed fitness.
- Species/cytotype/habitat/flowering stage explains all apparent O × S variation: within-population covarying-adaptation claim fails.
- Enemy-shielding route exists but affects parasitoid fitness without changing plant seed output: interesting three-trophic ecology, but no adaptive claim for the plant.
- A model with ordinary head-centred encounters explains all observation: world-relative orientation-as-filter mechanism unsupported.
- Natural selection exists but no heritable/head-history evidence: call it current ecology/selection, not historical coevolution.

## Claim hierarchy

1. **Existing literature:** head-centred multitrophic communities and separate pollinator, seed-feeder, parasitoid mechanisms are real in published Cirsium; Agrawal's selective spine effect is established in Centaurea.
2. **Current user datasets:** macro image proxies, discrete orientation/phyllary/sticky histories, antagonist fitness costs, BITA selectivity theory. No direct current O × true-spine × seed predator × parasitoid fitness dataset.
3. **Prospective ecological discovery:** dual sign switch of a true head structural defence by pollinator/seed-antagonist/parasitoid entry routing and early-versus-late attack stage, tested with viable-achene consequences.
4. **Prospective evolutionary discovery:** replication of the above across independent populations with genetic covariance/ancestral state evidence, linking selective guild regimes to actual evolved trait combinations.
5. **Not claimed:** any Japanese Cirsium head is currently demonstrated to use a parasitoid shelter paradox, to evolve spine/angle jointly, or to be adapted to a specific insect assemblage.
